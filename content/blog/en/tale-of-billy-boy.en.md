# The Tale of Billy Boy

## The Boy

There was once a boy called Billy. By all accounts, he was a decent lad. Quiet, hardworking, careful with what little he had. While the other boys spent their coins on sweets and games, Billy saved his.

A penny became a shilling. A shilling became a pound. Then Billy stopped keeping money because he needed it and started keeping money because he loved it. He worked, traded, invested, bought property, lent money, made good deals and, later, perhaps a few bad ones too. Nobody could say exactly when the sensible boy became the man who measured nearly everything in profit, but by middle age Billy Boy was rich. Very rich.

He had houses, land, accounts, business interests and, according to the old story, a considerable amount of gold. The richer Billy became, the less he trusted anyone with the full picture. Not banks, not lawyers, and certainly not business partners. Not even his own family.

So he did what men had done for centuries before passwords and private keys existed: he hid part of his fortune. Somewhere beyond the town, on land only he knew properly, Billy buried what he considered too valuable to entrust to anybody else. And because Billy was no fool, he made a map.

## The Map

There were landmarks, distances, an old wall, a tree, perhaps a well. Enough to find the place again. There was only one problem — Billy kept the map as secret as the treasure. He always intended to explain everything later. But later is a dangerous place to keep important plans.

Billy died suddenly before he could. His family found papers, keys, contracts and accounts. They inherited whatever could be identified and proved. But the map was gone. Maybe it was hidden too well. Maybe it was thrown away with old papers. Maybe it remained inside some forgotten book. Nobody knows. And nobody was looking, because nobody knew it existed. So the end of Billy Boy became the beginning of the legend of Billy Boy's treasure.

## The Treasure

Years later, one of Billy's old houses stood abandoned and half-ruined. While clearing it out, somebody found an old map hidden behind a loose board. Time had not been kind to it. The paper was stained and torn, some of the writing had disappeared, and the landmarks no longer matched anything people could identify with certainty. The map passed from hand to hand and was sold more than once. With every new owner came a new theory about the missing gold. Before long, treasure hunters, gamblers, and rather shady gentlemen were digging around old walls, dry wells, and forgotten pieces of land. One generation passed the story on to the next. Strangers argued over where Billy Boy had buried his fortune, while the people he had actually loved had never received it.
Billy spent his whole life making money. And in the end, he protected it so well that the wrong people spent decades looking for it, while the right people were left even without the knowledge of it.

Today, most of us do not bury gold in fields. Our treasure is buried behind passwords and seed phrases, inside cryptocurrency wallets and cloud accounts. But the problem has hardly changed. Giving a seed phrase to another person means that person can use it today. Putting a password into a document creates another copy that can be stolen. But keeping everything only in your own head creates the same problem Billy Boy had.

So how do you leave the map without giving the treasure away too soon? That is the idea behind **The Digital Heir**.

You place the important information inside an encrypted Envelope and choose how the system should check whether you are still around. As long as you respond, nothing is released. If you stop responding to the sequence of checks you configured, your chosen heir receives the opportunity to unlock the information intended for them. Nobody needs to know that your map or treasure even exists. No one needs your seed phrase in advance.

Billy Boy had the treasure and he had the map. What he never found was the last piece between the two: **a safe way to pass the map to the right person at the right time, when he could no longer do it himself.**
